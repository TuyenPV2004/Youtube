**Central question:** How can a bird sit on an energized wire unharmed, and what changes when the same perch becomes deadly?

**One-sentence answer:** A bird is usually safe because its two feet touch nearby points at nearly the same electrical potential; danger appears when its body bridges points at meaningfully different potentials.

**Editorial promise:** Begin with a calm, visible contradiction; reject two familiar shortcuts; reveal contact geometry as the hidden mechanism; then turn that mechanism into the title’s exception.

## Overall direction by scene

1. **Scene 1 — The system (00:00–00:08):** Start at neighborhood scale, not with another static bird close-up. Establish that the wire belongs to a working electrical system.
2. **Scene 2 — The contradiction (00:08–00:21):** Move to real birds, then one purposeful landing. The visual action is arrival and settling, not a repeated zoom toward feet.
3. **Scene 3 — Negative evidence (00:21–00:27):** Three fast observations—no flash, no shock, no reaction—turn stillness into a mystery.
4. **Scene 4 — The wrong answers (00:27–00:44):** Visualize the feather-shield myth, then the “electricity prefers the wire” shortcut, and decisively reject both.
5. **Scene 5 — The hidden mechanism (00:44–00:52):** Only now earn the close view of the contact points. Reveal the tiny distance and nearly equal potential with a clean motion graphic.
6. **Scene 6 — The geometry changes (00:52–00:59):** Return to real wing movement, then show two dangerous contact patterns as a diagram—not a fake electrocution reenactment.
7. **Scene 7 — Payoff and circular return (00:59–01:01):** Return to the wire image and complete the title with “UNTIL THEY DO.”
