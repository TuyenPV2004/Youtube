**Project status:** RESEARCH VERIFIED · SCRIPT APPROVED · VO GENERATED · MEDIA HOLD

**Central question:** Why can lightning return to the same object repeatedly instead of “using up” that location?

**One-sentence answer:** A previous strike does not protect a location; if its height, isolation and surrounding electric field again favor an upward connection to a descending leader, another discharge can attach there.

**Editorial promise:** Start with a real repeated strike, give the simple answer early, reveal the leader–streamer attachment process, distinguish repeated strokes from separate flashes, then return to the same tower with a new understanding.

## Overall direction by scene

1. **Repeated impact:** Show a real city or tower under repeated lightning; establish the myth.
2. **First payoff:** A strike does not make a place immune. The conditions that favored it may still exist.
3. **Build the storm:** Explain charge separation without turning the opening into a classroom lecture.
4. **The invisible search:** Reveal the descending stepped leader and upward streamers.
5. **The attachment:** Explain why tall, isolated objects often participate in the winning connection.
6. **Two kinds of repetition:** Separate multiple strokes in one flickering flash from separate flashes returning later.
7. **Test the variables:** Compare a tower, a low roof and open ground; height matters, but does not provide deterministic prediction.
8. **Real-world response:** Show how protection systems provide a preferred interception point and path to ground.
9. **Boundary and safety:** Lightning remains probabilistic and dangerous; do not turn the explanation into a strike-prediction trick.
10. **Circular return:** Revisit the opening tower. Lightning has no memory; the geometry still matters.
